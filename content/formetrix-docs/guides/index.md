
## **Goal-Driven Tracking in Formetrix**

Formetrix works best when tracking is **intentional**.
  
This guide explains how goals shape tracking behavior inside the app, how they influence interpretation, and how to avoid common mistakes that make progress harder to read.

Rather than chasing everything at once, Formetrix helps you focus on **what matters most in the phase you are in**.

---
## **Step 1 — Choose One Primary Goal (Mandatory)**

When creating a profile, you must select a primary goal:

- Fat Loss
    
- Muscle Gain
    
- Recomposition
    
- Performance
    
- Health

This choice is not a label. It is a **lens**.

> [!Important]
> You can improve multiple aspects of fitness at the same time, but you should **optimize for one goal at a time**.
>   
> Maximizing fat loss, muscle gain, performance, and recovery simultaneously often creates conflicting signals. Formetrix reduces that conflict by anchoring interpretation to a single goal.

You can change your goal later. What matters is that **during any given phase**, the system understands what progress should look like.

---
## **Step 2 — Let the Goal Shape Interpretation (Not the Data)**

The same measurement can mean very different things depending on your goal. For example:

- Stable weight can be **expected** during recomposition
    
- The same stability can indicate **insufficient stimulus** during muscle gain
    
- Short-term increases may be acceptable—or even expected—depending on context
  
Formetrix uses your goal to:

- prioritize relevant signals
    
- contextualize trends over time
    
- prevent overreaction to normal fluctuations

This does not hide information. It **changes how that information is read**.

>[!tip] **At a Glance — How Goals Change What Matters**
>
|**Goal**|**Primary Signals**|**Supporting Context**|**Lower Priority**|
|---|---|---|---|
|**Fat Loss**|Waist trend, body fat change|Weight trend, WHtR|Daily scale noise|
|**Muscle Gain**|FFMI, limb measurements|Weight trend|Short-term fat gain|
|**Recomposition**|Composition shift|Strength, symmetry|Scale movement|
|**Performance**|Output, capacity, balance|Recovery signals|Aesthetic changes|
|**Health**|Stability, risk-related markers|Waist, BMR|Short-term variation|

---
## **Step 3 — Measure Consistently, Not Aggressively**

More measurements do not automatically produce better insight.

What matters most is:

- similar conditions (time of day, hydration, posture)
    
- repeatable methods
    
- sufficient time between comparisons

> [!Tip]
> Consistency beats precision. A slightly imperfect method used consistently is more valuable than a perfect method used sporadically.

Your goal determines **what deserves attention**, not how often you should chase numbers.

---
## **Step 4 — Provide Context, Let the System Do the Work**

Your profile includes training, lifestyle, and goal details for a reason.

The more context you provide:

- the more accurate estimation becomes
    
- the clearer interpretation gets
    
- the more specialized the experience feels

[[formetrix-ai|Formetrix AI]] uses this context to **work toward your goal**, not just reflect measurements back to you.

Think of it as a system that learns **how to read your body**, not just record it.

---
## **Bottom Line**

Formetrix is designed to show **direction**, not daily verdicts.

Regardless of your goal:

- single readings are informational
    
- trends are actionable
    
- isolated changes are rarely meaningful

Progress becomes clear when tracking is **goal-aligned, contextual, and consistent**.
